## Ranked findings

### 1. The workflow applied fixed ceremony to a small change

**Observed impact:** 52.9 minutes of planning, 25.4 minutes of provisioning, and 27.0 minutes of manual-session preparation.
These categories total 105.2 minutes. The ledger records 17 gate presentations: three each for Gates 1–5, then one each for Gates 6–7.
These figures measure burden, not proven avoidable waste.

The human twice challenged overengineering. The architecture plan then decreased from 317 to 106 lines.
The required architecture output included a plan, an HTML card, and matching SVG views.
UX required two alternatives. The runner required a wizard and complete criterion mapping.
This structure encourages agents to produce every artifact regardless of decision value.

**Evidence:** chat turns 4–7, 16, and 29–30.
See [architect instructions](../../../.agents/skills/f-architect/SKILL.md), [runner instructions](../../../.agents/skills/f-runner/SKILL.md), and [ledger events](../../../.sdlc/advisor/20261017-CRM-014-sdlc-time-001/ledger-events-complete.json).

**Hypothesis:** Fixed role and artifact requirements explain part of the delay. Confidence: high for disproportionate artifacts, medium for attributable time.
Some of this work qualified a new pipeline. Not every minute in these categories can be removed.

### 2. Verification and setup defects created a recovery workflow larger than implementation

**Observed impact:** 107.7 recorded minutes across the first run/setup recovery and second run/acceptance correction.
This includes legitimate diagnosis and checks, rather than 107.7 minutes of worker failure.

Factory rejected a normal executable symlink under nested `ee-crm/node_modules` after the worker completed.
A direct scanner probe could reproduce the issue without another worker.
The first recovery report claimed no setup-only workaround existed.
An architect later proved a mount-based workaround without Factory changes.
The earlier categorical claim exceeded the available evidence.

A08 then required the whole page to fit at 320px. The baseline already overflowed.
Both attempts returned 94/95. Independent QA corrected feature attribution and rejected five deliberately broken layouts.
Run 003 passed 95/95 with the same candidate digest as run 002's last attempt.

**Evidence:** runs 001–003, [setup recovery](../../architecture/CRM-014-setup-recovery-001.md), and [QA scope correction](../../../.sdlc/CRM-014/verification-003/test-impact-addendum.md).
Both builder streams were read for all four attempts.
Run 001 includes recovered patch errors, but its worker exits 0. The controller scanner caused the interruption.

**Hypothesis:** Exact preflight and baseline attribution would prevent much of this recovery cycle.
Confidence: high for failure causes, medium for counterfactual savings.
A generic baseline GREEN and expected RED did not prove that every acceptance assertion was correctly scoped.

### 3. Reviews checked agreement with the contract but missed an incorrect product assumption

The approved contract placed System Logs under Teachers.
Acceptance and UX review accepted that hierarchy.
The human rejected it during manual review and also found missing separator spacing.
The human then clarified that top-level pages need no breadcrumb.

**Evidence:** chat turns 31–34, [first manual feedback](../../../.sdlc/CRM-014/revision-004/human-feedback.json), and the historical sealed contracts.
Current story documents reflect later corrections and cannot establish the earlier requirement by themselves.

**Observed impact:** 9.7 recorded minutes for feedback and corrections, plus earlier planning and review effort spent on an incorrect hierarchy.
The last direct correction turns took about 3.4 minutes combined, including focused browser checks.
These corrections are not equivalent in scope to the complete feature.

**Hypothesis:** Repeated specification checks allowed a shared assumption to pass through several roles.
Confidence: high for the escaped mismatch, medium for the cause.
Later feedback also refined an approved design. The evidence cannot determine the human's earlier unstated intent.

### 4. Each phase rediscovered operational details and repeated evidence

The test capability index was absent during planning.
Provisioning resolved model availability and runtime boundaries.
The runner then prepared synthetic login, app/session helpers, a mobile preview, and result capture.
The operator and runner reached usage limits, and the parent completed their work.

The ledger occupies 7,737,271 bytes.
Large evidence packages and resumed role work plausibly increase context and coordination burden.
No complete role token telemetry exists, so the report cannot quantify that cost.

**Evidence:** chat turns 4, 17–18, 23, and 29–30.
See the [dispatch card](../../../.sdlc/CRM-014/operator-003/gate-5-card.md) and `.sdlc/CRM-014/runner-001/`.

**Confidence:** High for repeated setup and large records. Medium for repeatability risk. Low for any specific token-saving estimate.

## Proposed improvements

All budgets below are experiment limits. They are not existing runtime settings or promised delivery times.

### P1. Introduce a small UI lane

**Target:** `docs/workflow/factory-sdlc.md`, orchestration protocol, and `f-orchestrator`.
**Owner:** Workflow maintainer.

Eligibility requires a local UI change with understood routes and reusable test/startup tooling.
Exclude authentication, permission, schema, external API, and deployment changes.
Unknown setup or uncertain product scope requires explicit escalation.

Proposed lane:

1. Prepare one short change brief with a route hierarchy table, one visual, affected files, and acceptance examples.
2. Obtain one combined product/design/architecture decision.
3. Let independent QA prepare focused assertions and relevant regression coverage through the reusable runtime.
4. Present the exact test contract, candidate, control, model, and budget for one combined test/dispatch decision.
5. Implement the change. Run independent evaluation and review. Present the running preview and findings.
6. Obtain one final human release decision after required human checks.

This retains seven logical assessments but proposes three human decision points.
The present workflow does not permit this consolidation. Approve the workflow change before a pilot.
A small-task lane can retain Factory execution and immutable controls. It does not require a new worker model.

**Experiment:** One documented policy proposal, then one eligible story.
**Budget:** At most 60 recorded agent-active minutes for the pilot, excluding human response gaps.
**Success:** At most three approval pauses, no unsupported hierarchy, complete independent checks, and explicit release authority.
**Stop:** Escalate if scope grows, setup is unknown, an independent check is unavailable, or the time limit is reached.
Preserve unfinished evidence instead of claiming completion.
**Approval route:** Human approves the revised operational policy before use. Existing stories retain their current gates.

### P2. Resolve product questions before producing detailed artifacts

**Target:** `f-ba`, `f-ux`, and `f-architect` instructions.
**Owner:** BA owns product meaning. UX owns the visual. Architect owns the short implementation plan.

Proposed instruction: “Record each affected route and its parent. Mark top-level routes. Resolve query handling and replaced controls before detailed planning.”
For local UI changes, produce one recommended visual. Produce alternatives only when a meaningful unresolved decision needs comparison.
Use one short plan. Require additional diagrams only when boundaries, data flow, or consequential interactions change.

For CRM-014, the table would identify Teachers and Logs as peers, with breadcrumbs only on schedule and day pages.
A focused product review must question whether the hierarchy makes sense. Criterion matching alone is insufficient.

**Experiment:** One navigation brief, before architecture work.
**Budget:** 10 minutes.
**Success:** Every affected route has an agreed parent and breadcrumb. The brief identifies defaults, removals, mobile behavior, and spacing.
**Stop:** Return unresolved product assumptions to the human before test authoring.
**Approval route:** Human approves instruction changes. Gates 1–3 still apply until P1 receives approval.

### P3. Qualify the application runtime once and reuse it

**Target:** `f-operator/provisioning.md`, `f-runner/session.md`, and application startup documentation.
**Owner:** Operator owns runtime qualification. Runner owns the reusable manual session.

Create one versioned startup recipe for dependencies, synthetic login/data, browser fixtures, readiness, private build output, and cleanup.
Execute the actual source scan and evaluator startup through the real mounted boundary before worker dispatch.
The runner should consume that same recipe.
Reuse qualification only when its relevant source, dependency, configuration, and boundary identities match.

**Experiment:** Replay the nested-app scanner failure and the qualified mounted setup without inference.
**Budget:** 45 minutes, zero worker runs.
**Success:** The old layout fails before paid dispatch. The qualified layout starts the evaluator and preview without repeated setup design.
**Stop:** Block readiness on source mismatch, protected writes, missing cleanup, or an unverifiable recipe.
**Approval route:** QA approves changed evaluator setup at Gate 4. Operator presents its identity at Gate 5.
Reusable infrastructure changes require their own review.

### P4. Attribute failures before requesting implementation repair

**Target:** `f-verificator`, test impact contract, and operator recovery procedure.
**Owner:** Verificator owns assertion scope. Operator owns failure triage.

Proposed instruction: “For each new invariant, record baseline behavior for its relevant non-feature conditions. Separate missing feature failures from existing defects.”
For layout, measure component containment and added document overflow.
Do not accept a missing-element RED result as proof that later geometry assertions are valid.
Use a small number of deliberate defects when an assertion's sensitivity is uncertain.

**Experiment:** Replay A08 with baseline, valid component, and five known defective layouts.
**Budget:** 30 minutes, zero worker runs.
**Success:** Existing header overflow does not fail the breadcrumb contract. Actual clipping, overflow, and unusable links still fail.
**Stop:** Retain existing coverage if replacement assertions cannot detect the intended failures.
**Approval route:** Renew Gate 4 for changed assertions and Gate 5 for the new control.

For uncertain setup, separately pilot `task.budget.maxAttempts: 1`.
A new run requires diagnosed repairability and renewed approval.
This can add a pause for a genuine code defect. Compare that tradeoff rather than applying the policy universally.
The installed CLI offers no advisor-controlled pause inside an already approved automatic retry loop.

### P5. Review the visible change early and keep manual testing focused

**Target:** Post-green review contract, `f-review`, `f-ux`, and `f-runner`.
**Owner:** UX reviewer owns visible product checks. Runner owns human checks.

Show the actual affected route, including mobile, before preparing a custom manual-testing application.
Use a reusable session and result recorder.
Map automated evidence to criteria. Reserve manual checks for visual judgment, realistic interaction, and unresolved risks.
Use a new shell wizard only when it solves a human-only procedure that the standard session cannot support.

**Experiment:** Replay this story's final visual review using the shared preview and a short checklist.
**Budget:** 10 minutes of session preparation, zero worker runs.
**Success:** The human sees the Logs hierarchy and separator spacing directly. No bespoke session tool is required.
**Stop:** Keep a manual check when automation cannot establish its required result.
**Approval route:** Human approves changes to runner and review contracts. Gate 7 remains the release authority.

### P6. Bound documentation and correction work

**Target:** Gate-card templates, ledger serialization, and post-review correction procedure.
**Owner:** Orchestrator and workflow maintainer. QA owns changed acceptance expectations.

Use cards below 150 words with the decision, behavior delta, checks, blockers, and an isolated diff.
Keep full evidence behind links.
Store immutable snapshots once and reference their digests. Do not repeat multi-megabyte manifests in routine handoffs.

Define a correction path that records the requirement delta, affected tests, final source identity, independent review, and explicit delivery authorization.
A changed hierarchy requires product and QA agreement. A spacing correction needs focused visual/regression checks.
Reuse unaffected evidence only after checking its dependency scope.
Do not describe the old full Factory result as verification of changed final source.

**Experiment:** Read-only replay of the final two corrections and their closure.
**Budget:** 45 minutes, zero worker runs.
**Success:** One correction summary identifies changed criteria, current checks, stale evidence, and required decisions.
Target a current-state handoff below 20 KB while retaining all original snapshots.
**Stop:** Stop if evidence authenticity, scope detection, or truthful release status changes.
**Approval route:** Human approves the correction policy and ledger contract. Existing control versions remain immutable.
